# The update of 2026-11-24

What changed in the Furnisher on 2026-11-24, why, where it lives, how to see it, what it measured, and how to take it back. Written for the repository's author and for a coding agent that opens the repository without any other context.

## Who and why

The changes were made by Martin Bielik's SpatialTimber Composer project, with an AI coding agent (Claude), in two rounds on the same day. The Composer generates and checks apartment layouts and judges every apartment with this program: it sends each apartment's rooms to the CLI and accepts an apartment only when every room is furnished and the apartment's score (the room scores weighted by area) is at least 90.

Small rooms often failed for reasons that lay in the search or the library, not in the room: a 1-room bathroom was given the 3-room bathtub, a bedroom too narrow for the 2.00 m double bed with its free strips got no bed at all, a shower that fits in the other corner was never tried there. The update keeps every room that was furnished before exactly as it was and gives the others a reasonable way to be furnished, at a small, visible cost in points.

All measurements below were made on 18,731 apartment requests the Composer had sent (78,116 rooms), and on a part of the Neufert benchmark; the data of these measurements is with Martin Bielik.

## How a room is furnished now

`furnishRoom` in `furnisher-engine/src/engine/furnish.ts` tries these ways, in this order, and uses the first that places every piece of the room:

1. The regular pieces of `placement_order.md` at their ranked positions. A step with alternatives (`Bathtube | Shower`) takes the first alternative the library has for exactly the apartment type (`resolveStep` in `library/lookup.ts`). Positions are ranked by `sortOptionsByScore`: each piece's positions scored with the later pieces at their first position, the best kept.
2. The regular pieces at their other positions (`searchOtherPositions`).
3. The step's other alternative, looked up the way it was before 2026-11-24 (the first alternative found for any apartment type; today this is the 3-room bathtub in a 1- or 2-room bathroom), at its ranked positions, then at its other positions. No points off.
4. Each rule of `fallbacks.json` for the room and apartment type, in file order: the regular piece replaced by a smaller one, at ranked positions, then at other positions. `penaltyPoints` (2) off the room's score.

When none places every piece, the result of 1 stands, as before. A room that 1 furnishes completely never reaches 2 to 4, so it is computed exactly as before.

Then `scoreFurnishedRoom` gives the room's score (`scoreRoom` less the reductions) and whether it counts as furnished, and `summarizeApartment` the apartment's score and whether every room counts as furnished.

## (a) The shower and the smaller fallback pieces (first round: `8491647`, `131daab`, `8130eaf`)

**Before.** `findFurnitureByName` falls back to the nearest higher apartment type, so for `Bathtube | Shower` the bathtub was found (for type 3) before the shower was tried: 1- and 2-room bathrooms got the 1.70 m bathtub. A room where one piece found no place stayed incomplete.

**Now.** `resolveStep` takes the alternative the library has for exactly the apartment type, so 1- and 2-room bathrooms get their 0.90 m shower; where the shower leaves the room incomplete, the bathtub is tried (way 3 above). The smaller pieces are ordinary library entries made by `furn_library/make_fallback_pieces.py` (every existing entry unchanged) and are used through the rules of `furnisher-engine/src/library/default/fallbacks.json`:

| Rule | Room, apartment types | Piece used instead |
|---|---|---|
| `bathtub-160` | Bathroom, 1 to 4 | Bathtube160 and Sinc112: bathtub 1.60 m and washbasin free area 1.12 m deep (instead of 1.70 m and 1.15 m) |
| `children-bed-end-80` | Children, 1 to 4 | Bed295: free strip of 0.80 m at the bed's end (instead of 0.85 m) |
| `kitchen-220` | Kitchen, 1 | Kitchen220: counter 2.20 m (instead of 2.40 m) |
| `kitchen-L-230` | Kitchen, 2 and 3 | Kitchen230: L with a long leg of 2.30 m (instead of 2.40 m) |
| `kitchen-of-3-rooms` | Kitchen, 4 | the kitchen of a 3-room apartment |
| `kitchen-of-3-rooms-L-230` | Kitchen, 4 | the 3-room kitchen with the 2.30 m leg |
| `double-bed-160` | Bedroom, 1 to 4 | Bed160: double bed 1.60 m wide with 0.60 m beside it (instead of 2.00 m with 0.80 m) |

A room furnished by a rule loses `penaltyPoints` = 2 points of its 0 to 100 score, so the larger piece always wins where it fits.

**Measured.** All 56,970 rooms furnished completely before, outside 1- and 2-room bathrooms, kept their answer byte for byte. Of 10,866 bathrooms of 1- and 2-room apartments furnished before, 10,295 got the shower and 571 kept the bathtub through way 3; none was lost. Of 8,498 rooms incomplete before, 3,025 were furnished (most often by `double-bed-160`, 1,635 rooms). The 2 points were chosen on 1,782 complete apartments that use a fallback: 7 of them drop below 90 at 2 points (31 at 5). The Neufert benchmark (its first 3,000 of 15,237 requests): all 11,185 rooms furnished before outside 1- and 2-room bathrooms identical. The batch took 5.4 % longer.

## (b) One way to furnish a room for engine, app and CLI (`1d07459`)

**Before.** The search (`sortOptionsByScore`), the strict clearance check, the shower lookup and the fallbacks lived in the CLI's `core.ts`; the app had its own greedy search (`findBestInitialResult` in `App.tsx`) and looked up pieces itself with `findFurnitureByName`, so the app still gave 1-room bathrooms the bathtub and knew no fallbacks.

**Now.** All of it lives in `furnisher-engine/src/engine/furnish.ts` (`furnishRoom`, `sortOptionsByScore`, `scoreFurnishedRoom`) and `pipeline.ts`, which resolves every step with `resolvePipelineStep` (the fallback piece in force, otherwise `resolveStep`) and carries the CLI's warning for a piece whose every placement interferes with a clearance zone (the best one is then kept anyway). The CLI's `core.ts` calls `furnishRoom` with `polygonSimplified: true` (it simplifies the polygon itself, once, also for its door output). The app's `doFurnish` calls `furnishRoom`, then `runRoomPipelineAt` at the chosen selection, so its variant controls keep working on positions in generation order; the room keeps its `fallback`, which the variant controls and the manual drop pass on as `pieces`, so a fallback piece stays in place while the user edits.

**Fallbacks and custom setups.** The engine applies `fallbacks.json` only when the caller gives neither its own `library` nor its own `pipeline`, or passes `fallbacks` explicitly. The app passes them for every room whose pipeline the user has not customised in the sidebar (`isSectionCustomized`), and not the kitchen rules when the flexible kitchen is on. A customised room is furnished as the user set it up.

**How to see it.** `cd furnisher-for-rhino/engine-cli; npm run build; npm test`: 16 rooms from real apartments (`test/cases.json`), among them a 1-room bathroom (shower), a 3 x 4 bathroom (bathtub 1.60 m), a bedroom 4 squares wide (bed 1.60 m), furnished the app's way and by the CLI, equal in everything compared.

**Measured.** On 3,122 of the 18,731 requests (every 6th), all 12,956 rooms of the CLI's answers are byte for byte those of `8130eaf`; the full single-request answers of 150 requests are identical.

## (c) Other positions before another piece (`69725b9`)

**Before.** All positions of the shower in a small bathroom tie in the ranking; the first is kept, and toilet and washbasin then find no place, so the bathroom took the bathtub, although the shower in the other corner leaves room for both.

**Now.** `searchOtherPositions` in `furnish.ts`: where a way leaves a step without a place at its ranked positions, the same pieces are tried at their other positions, depth first, each step's positions in ranked order; the first complete layout wins. Bound: `SEARCH_RUNS` = 300 pipeline runs per way (`FurnishOptions.searchRuns` per call, 0 switches the search off). Order of attempts: the pieces at their ranked positions, at their other positions, the next alternative (ranked, then other positions), the fallback pieces in file order (each ranked, then other positions).

**Measured.** Of the 571 bathrooms that kept the bathtub, 549 have a complete shower layout (found in at most 32 runs, median 13, at most 0.93 s per room); 22 have none (all 1.725 x 2.35 m inside; the washbasin finds no place in any shower and toilet layout), and keep the bathtub. On all 18,731 requests: all 68,302 rooms furnished completely at their ranked positions before are byte for byte unchanged; bathrooms changed: 549 bathtub to shower, 59 bathtub 1.60 m to the regular 1.70 m bathtub at another position, 84 bathtub 1.60 m to the 1.70 m bathtub through the next alternative, 215 incomplete bathrooms now furnished; no room of another type changes. Apartments complete with a score of at least 90: 13,578 before, 13,713 after (139 gained, 4 lost: 2-room apartments just above 90 whose bathroom goes from the bathtub, 100, to the shower layout found, which gives the washbasin one position, 91.7).

## (d) The apartment's score and "every room furnished" (`a8744b4`, `0fc94c7`)

**Before.** The engine and the CLI gave room scores; a program using the CLI had to compute the apartment's score and decide itself whether each room was furnished.

**Now.** In `furnish.ts`:

- `roomFurnished`: the room has steps, every step has a placement (a valid one, or one placed by hand in the app), and no two pieces overlap.
- `pieceOverlaps`: two pieces overlap when their solid outlines (the closed outlines of their geometry) share more than `OVERLAP_M2` = 0.01 m2. This happens where every placement of a piece interferes with a clearance zone and the pipeline keeps the best one anyway (the warning of section b), for example the dining table on the sofa.
- `summarizeApartment`: the room scores weighted by the room areas, rounded to 2 decimals (ties rounded to even, as Python's `round` does), and whether every room counts as furnished.

The CLI weights each room by its `area` when the request gives one, otherwise by the area of its polygon; every room of the request counts (send only the rooms that should). The app shows the summary under "Furnish apartment", with the drawn rooms' areas.

**Measured.** On the 3,122 requests, the apartment score equals the Composer's own computation from the same answer on all 3,122, to the last decimal; "every room furnished" equals its judgement except in the 35 1-room apartments of section e. Among the 3,122 requests one room has overlapping pieces (the living room of a 4-room apartment, sofa and dining table sharing 0.03 m2); it does not count as furnished on either side.

## (e) The living room of a 1-room apartment without its dining table (`a0d923d`)

**Before.** The single room of a 1-room apartment is furnished as a `Living room` with apartment type 1: Sofa and Dining, weights 0.5 and 0.5. A table that fits nowhere left the room incomplete and scored it 50 (with a sofa of two or more positions).

**Now.** `fallbacks.json` lists steps a room may do without, by room and apartment type:

```json
"optionalPenaltyPoints": 10,
"optional": [
  { "id": "no-dining-table", "room": "Living room", "aptTypes": [1], "furniture": "Dining", "label": "no dining table" }
]
```

When the optional steps are the only ones without a place, the room is scored on its other steps (their weights rescaled) less `optionalPenaltyPoints` and counts as furnished (`scoreFurnishedRoom`). In every other apartment type and room nothing changes: a living room of a 2-room apartment still needs its table. `placement_order.md` names this in its editing rules.

**Measured.** On the 3,122 requests (1,084 1-room apartments): 35 living rooms miss only the table; each goes from 50 to 90; the table's cost to the apartment's score falls from 19.6 / 25.0 / 30.6 points (min / median / max) to 3.9 / 5.0 / 6.1; 30 of the 35 apartments are then furnished with a score of at least 90. Apartments of 2 or more rooms: every room identical to the build before this change.

## The CLI's answer: new fields

Per room:

| Field | When | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `furnished` | always | The room counts as furnished (section d; with section e). |
| `fallback` | the room used way 3 or 4 | `{id, label, penaltyPoints, pieces: [{furnitureName, replaces}], scoreBeforePenalty}`; `id` is a rule of `fallbacks.json` or `next-alternative`. |
| `steps[].replaces` | a step with a fallback piece | The regular piece it stands in for, e.g. `Bathtube160` replaces `Bathtube`. |
| `missingOptional` | an optional step found no place | `{pieces: [{furnitureName, id, label}], penaltyPoints, scoreBeforePenalty}`. |
| `overlaps` | two pieces overlap | `[{a, b, m2}]`. |

Per answer: `apartment: {score, furnished}`. Per request room, the optional input `area` (m2) weighs the room in the apartment's score. The batch runner (`furnisher-batch.cjs`) passes all of these through and computes `apartment` after its retries.

A real answer (a 1-room apartment; `geometry`, `bbox`, `smallBbox`, `doors`, `selectedIndex`, `variantGroups` and `stepScores` left out):

Request:

```json
{"rooms": [
  {"name": "Living room", "polygon": [[0.0, 5.625], [3.75, 5.625], [3.75, 7.5], [0.0, 7.5]], "windows": [], "area": 7.031},
  {"name": "Kitchen", "polygon": [[0.0, 3.125], [2.5, 3.125], [2.5, 5.625], [0.0, 5.625]], "windows": [], "area": 6.25},
  {"name": "Bathroom", "polygon": [[0.0, 1.25], [2.5, 1.25], [2.5, 3.125], [0.0, 3.125]], "windows": [], "area": 4.688}],
 "doors": [[2.813, 7.5], [1.25, 5.625], [3.125, 5.625], [1.25, 1.25]], "aptType": 1}
```

Answer (shortened):

```json
{"aptType": 1,
 "rooms": [
  {"name": "Living room", "score": 90, "furnished": true,
   "steps": [{"furnitureName": "Sofa", "optionCount": 2}, {"furnitureName": "Dining", "optionCount": 0}],
   "missingOptional": {"pieces": [{"furnitureName": "Dining", "id": "no-dining-table", "label": "no dining table"}], "penaltyPoints": 10, "scoreBeforePenalty": 100}},
  {"name": "Kitchen", "score": 100, "furnished": true, "steps": [{"furnitureName": "Kitchen", "optionCount": 6}]},
  {"name": "Bathroom", "score": 100, "furnished": true,
   "steps": [{"furnitureName": "Shower", "optionCount": 7}, {"furnitureName": "Toilet", "optionCount": 7}, {"furnitureName": "Sinc", "optionCount": 2}]}],
 "apartment": {"score": 96.09, "furnished": true}}
```

## What a person sees in the app

- A 1- or 2-room bathroom gets the shower; where no shower layout exists, the bathtub.
- A room furnished with a smaller piece shows that piece; selecting the room in furnish mode opens its panel, which names it under the score, e.g. "bathtub 1.60 m and washbasin free area 1.12 m deep (instead of 1.70 m and 1.15 m) · 2 points off". A 1-room living room without its table shows "no dining table · 10 points off" there and scores 90.
- The sidebar's pipeline editor shows the piece a step uses for the apartment type (Shower in a 1-room bathroom, not Bathtube).
- Under "Furnish apartment": "Apartment score … · every room furnished" (or "not every room furnished").

## What did not change

- Every room that its regular pieces furnish completely at their ranked positions keeps its layout byte for byte (68,302 rooms of the 18,731 requests; section c).
- The app's manual editing: variant scrolling, dragging a piece to another wall, the candidates for unplaced pieces, the flexible kitchen. The only difference: in a room furnished with a fallback piece, scrolling and dragging keep that piece, and the pieces after a dragged one are looked up the way the pipeline looks them up (`resolvePipelineStep`).
- Custom pipelines and custom libraries: a room whose pipeline is customised in the app, and any `library` or `pipeline` passed to the engine, is furnished without fallbacks and optional steps unless they are passed as `fallbacks`. Two things apply to them too: a step `A | B` takes the alternative the library has for exactly the apartment type (what `placement_order.md` always described), with the other alternative as the next way, and the search for other positions.
- The furniture library's existing entries; the score formula (`scorer.ts`); the request format (only `area` is new and optional).

## Parameters a person may want to change

| Parameter | Where | Today |
|---|---|---|
| `penaltyPoints` | `furnisher-engine/src/library/default/fallbacks.json` | 2 points off a room furnished by a fallback rule |
| `optionalPenaltyPoints` | same file | 10 points off a room without an optional piece |
| a rule's `aptTypes`, an optional step's `aptTypes` | same file | e.g. `[1]`: the table may be missing only in 1-room apartments |
| the order of the rules | same file | the first rule that furnishes the room wins |
| `SEARCH_RUNS` | `furnisher-engine/src/engine/furnish.ts` (per call: `FurnishOptions.searchRuns`) | 300 pipeline runs per way; 0 switches the search for other positions off |
| `OVERLAP_M2` | `furnisher-engine/src/engine/furnish.ts` | 0.01 m2 shared floor before two pieces count as overlapping |

After changing any of them, rebuild the CLI (`npm run build` in `furnisher-for-rhino/engine-cli`) and run `npm test`; the app picks them up with its next build or dev-server reload.

## Not verified

- The app was looked at in a browser on one case (a 1-room apartment with a 2.5 x 1.875 m bathroom and a 5.0 x 4.375 m living room: shower, toilet, washbasin; sofa and dining table; "Apartment score 100.0 · every room furnished"). The notes for a fallback piece and for the missing table were not seen on screen; they are checked by the type check and by `npm test` only.
- The final build (`0fc94c7`) was measured on 3,122 of the 18,731 requests; the build before sections d and e on all 18,731.
- The Neufert benchmark was not rerun in full (only its first 3,000 requests, after the first round).

## How to take a change back

Tried on a copy of this branch: the later commits build on the earlier ones, so revert the newest first, as a group. Each group below reverts without conflicts, and after it the CLI builds, `npm test` passes and the app type-checks.

| To take back | Revert | Result |
|---|---|---|
| (d) the overlap rule only | `git revert 0fc94c7` | rooms with overlapping pieces count as furnished again |
| (e) the 1-room living room without its table | `git revert 0fc94c7 a0d923d` | the table is required again (also takes back the overlap rule) |
| (d) and (e) | `git revert 0fc94c7 a0d923d a8744b4` | no `furnished`, no `apartment` in the answer |
| (c) the search for other positions, with (d) and (e) | `git revert 0fc94c7 a0d923d a8744b4 69725b9` | the bathtub again where the shower is found only at another position |
| everything of the second round | `git revert 0fc94c7 a0d923d a8744b4 69725b9 1d07459` | the engine, app and CLI sources are exactly those of `8130eaf` again |

Reverting `a0d923d`, `a8744b4`, `69725b9` or `1d07459` alone stops with conflicts. Without reverting anything, the behaviour can also be switched off by data or options: remove the entry from `optional` in `fallbacks.json` (section e), set `SEARCH_RUNS` in `furnish.ts` to 0, or pass `searchRuns: 0` to `furnishRoom` (section c), or remove a rule from `rules` (section a). The first round (`8491647` to `8130eaf`) was not tried for reverting.
